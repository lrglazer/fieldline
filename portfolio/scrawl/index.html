What should it write?

Upload a PDF, or type and paste your text. Math goes between dollar signs, and chemistry uses \ce{…}. Typed PDFs work best. A scanned PDF is just a picture, so there’s no text to pull out.

From PDFs
Unicode like x², H₂O, ∫, ΔH and → is understood automatically.
Tables (ICE tables, data tables)
Start each row with |: | | HA | H^+ | A^- | then | I | 0.10 | 0 | 0 |. Tables in PDFs are detected automatically.
Layout
A blank line starts a new paragraph. Use --- on its own line for a page break.

How to get your handwriting into Scrawl

About ten minutes, once. After that, every PDF you convert comes out in your handwriting.

1. Print the template

Click Download template in step 1 and print it on letter paper at 100% / actual size, not “fit to page.”

2. Fill it in with a dark pen

Use black or dark blue ink. Write each character once, sitting on the blue line, at your normal size. Tails of g, p and y go below the line. Stay inside the box.

3. Take one clear photo

Lay the page flat in daylight and hold your phone straight above it. All four black corner squares must be in the shot, with no shadows on the page.

4. Upload and check

Drop the photo into step 1. Your letters show up in the grid. Tap any character that came out wrong to redraw it on screen.

Tips for the most realistic result
  • The last rows of the template are second versions of common letters and numbers. Write them naturally, not copies of the first ones. That variety makes the page look real.
  • Print or semi-print handwriting looks most natural. Connected cursive won’t join between letters.
  • Fill in the math and chemistry boxes too (Δ, √, ∫, →, ⇌, α, π). Any blanks fall back to the sample style.
  • Your handwriting is saved in this browser only. To use it on another device, upload the same photo there.
  • Typed (not scanned) PDFs work best: answer keys, worksheets, lecture notes. Tables such as ICE tables are detected and drawn as hand-ruled grids.

It reads your document

Text is pulled out of the PDF line by line. Exponents, subscripts, fractions, roots and chemical formulas are laid out the way you would write them.

Every letter gets a slightly different size, tilt and spacing, and lines drift a little, so the page never looks stamped.
