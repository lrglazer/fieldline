Help

Common mistakes (don't do these)

  • Copying sentences from the example or the sample report. Rewrite everything.
  • Forgetting units. Every number needs N, m, N·m, kg or degrees.
  • Calling his weight an in-plane force. Weight goes into the mat, not along it.
  • Saying "ball and socket" for the ankle. It's a hinge (talocrural) plus the subtalar joint.
  • Mixing up who is who: she attacks, his leg gets locked.
  • Using a source but not citing it with [#] in the sentence.
  • Wrong file names. Use the exact names in the tracker.
  • Changing someone else's section in the group doc. Leave a comment instead.

Words you'll see · flash cards, tap to flip

FrameTAP TO FLIP
A structure with at least one member that has 3 or more forces on it. Ours has 8 members.
MemberTAP TO FLIP
One rigid piece, like his thigh or her trunk.
Free body diagram (FBD)TAP TO FLIP
A drawing of one piece by itself with every force and moment on it.
Pin jointTAP TO FLIP
A connection that stops sliding but lets it rotate. 2 unknowns (x and y).
Smooth contactTAP TO FLIP
Two surfaces pushing with no friction. 1 unknown, straight out from the surface.
Two-force memberTAP TO FLIP
A piece with forces at only 2 points, so the force acts along the line between them. Our muscles.
Moment (N·m)TAP TO FLIP
A twisting effect: force × perpendicular distance.
BeamTAP TO FLIP
A long member with 3+ forces. The rubric needs at least 2. We have 7.
EversionTAP TO FLIP
The ankle rolling so the sole turns outward. Her lean-back does this to his ankle.
AdductorsTAP TO FLIP
Inner-thigh muscles that squeeze the legs together. Her squeeze force, T_A.
Shear (V) and moment (M) diagramTAP TO FLIP
A graph of the inside forces along a beam. Ours is his shin, for extra credit.
SolvableTAP TO FLIP
Unknowns ≤ independent equations. Ours: 19 ≤ 21.

Things the final rubric needs that are easy to miss
  • Write the equations out. Solver numbers alone lose points. Every member needs ΣFx = 0, ΣFy = 0 and ΣM = 0 written out, then solved.
  • Units and directions on every answer, like "K_Lx = 117.8 N →".
  • A section-cut FBD of his shin for the internal loads, separate from the member FBDs.
  • Assumptions from multiple sources, with realistic weights and measurements cited.
  • Conclusions compare to literature values, for example our ankle moment vs. the 47 N·m limit [2].
  • Show the professor's feedback was applied to the problem description.

Rubric

The professor's exact rubric for the problem description: 15 points, 5 per section. Each section needs all 5 of its criteria to get full points.

The assignment

Choose a static biomechanical system (human or animal) that can be modeled as a frame or machine, with at least 3 members, 2 of them beams (3+ forces). Give a short description of the system and what we plan to solve, and a schematic of the frame or machine.

Ours goes beyond this: 8 members, 7 beams, full FBDs and a solvability check.
